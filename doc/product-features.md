# Product features

## Product promise

Tickist gives projects, priorities, and deadlines one calm home. The intended user outcome is not merely storing tasks; it is knowing which next action deserves attention.

The public landing page describes the product in plain terms: a simplified, faithful preview of the dashboard (only features that exist), the main capabilities, and the fact that Tickist is free, open source and has no paid features. It loads none of the workspace code; `/app` and its dashboard are lazy routes, so visitors download only the landing page, routing and the sign-in session check. It also introduces [Spendist](https://spendist.app/), a related open-source application for tracking spending, recurring payments, and everyday personal finances.

## Public blog

Tickist has a public, responsive blog for visitors who are not signed in. The blog indexes are available in English (`/en/blog`) and Polish (`/pl/blog`). Each language has an independent editorial catalogue: posts are not automatically translated or paired across languages.

Posts, categories, and tags are published from the repository rather than an administration panel. The initial blog has no articles and no comments. Future public article pages may offer social sharing using their canonical URL.

## Inbox and projects

- Each account has one Inbox for uncategorised tasks.
- Each account starts with Work and Private workspaces. Existing projects are assigned to Private. Users can create and rename workspaces in Settings; the navigation switcher beside the avatar offers All and each workspace, with a fixed-width label on desktop and an icon on mobile. The selection filters project and task views, dashboard, tags, tree, statistics, and the project lists in the task editor, the task card's move-to-project picker, and the parent-project picker; an item's current project or parent stays listed even when it belongs to another workspace. Inbox and notifications remain available across workspaces.
- Each owned project belongs to one workspace; subprojects use their parent's workspace and move with it. A recipient of a shared project assigns it independently to one of their own workspaces, initially Private.
- Projects can be nested to represent outcomes and their component work.
- Selecting a project shows tasks from that project and all nested subprojects. When the selected project has descendants, independent checkboxes beside the selected project and each descendant can include or exclude that project's own tasks. All projects in the hierarchy are included by default, and the selection resets when the user moves to another project.
- Projects have names, descriptions, colours, icons, defaults, and a simple or extended task-card view. Branding offers 487 tree-shaken Lucide icons grouped into 18 project categories (Work & business, Software & tech, Home & DIY, Money & finance, Family & people, Health & fitness, Learning & study, Travel & outdoors, Events & celebrations and others). Search matches English names, keywords (for example "renovation" or "wedding") and categories; every word must match. Icon keys are stored on the project, so existing keys are never renamed or removed, and an unknown key shows a folder.
- The sidebar groups active work and special planning buckets such as Someday/Maybe, routine reminders, weekdays, and future work.
- **Weekdays** lists today and the next six days with the number of open tasks dated each day, counted in the account timezone. Opening a day shows two sections: **Scheduled for this day** (tasks scheduled on that day) and **Deadlines on this day** (deadlines due on that day). Pinned tasks belong to today: Today's first section is **Scheduled and pinned for today** and also lists pinned tasks dated another day or not at all, and Today's count includes them. Both sections match the day's count in the sidebar. The day heading and each section heading summarise their tasks: the count, the total estimate and the total time spent (`3 tasks · 2h 30m est. · 45m spent`); estimate and spent time appear only when above zero. A month view shows the same summary for its tasks.
- **Day plan:** in a day view and in the dashboard Today group, tasks can be put in a manual order with the drag handle beside each card (mouse or touch; the task area scrolls while a card is held near its top or bottom edge) or from the keyboard (Space or Enter on the handle to pick up, arrow keys to move, Space or Enter to drop, Escape to cancel); each move is announced to screen readers. The plan is stored per day in this browser (`tickist.day-order.v1` in local storage, kept for a week after the day), is shared by Weekdays → Today and the dashboard Today group, and can be cleared with Reset order. Tasks without a place in the plan follow it: scheduled tasks by time and priority, deadlines by due date and priority.
- Project owners can share a project by invitation. Email invitations reach only confirmed Tickist accounts; an address without an account receives no email. The owner sees every invitation as "Invited" until it is accepted, cancelled, or expires after 30 days, and cannot tell whether the address has an account or whether the person declined. Owners can cancel an invitation from the project's sharing tab, send or renew at most 20 invitations per hour, and an address invited within the last 24 hours is not re-invited to the same project. Recipients accept or decline from the Team view; accepted members can leave shared projects. Direct sharing without an invitation is limited to people the owner already collaborates with. Connected apps (MCP clients) cannot send, accept, or cancel invitations.
- MCP clients can create and move nested projects, recursively list a selected project's tasks, and delete owned non-Inbox projects. Deleting a project leaves its tasks unattached and promotes its direct children to root projects.

## Tasks

### Capture and structure

Tasks hold a title, description, project, priority, type, a date with an optional time, estimated and spent time, tags, assignees, subtasks, and reminders. Quick entry is available within task lists; full create/edit flows expose the wider task model. The estimate is empty unless the user enters one; opening or editing a task never adds a default estimate. A task date is either a **Deadline** (finish by that day, the default, shown with a flag icon) or **Scheduled** (do it on that day, shown with a calendar-check icon); the project editor's default task date uses the same two choices. Editing an existing task saves valid changes automatically after a short pause, including choices across tabs; reminders save separately once loaded and complete. Closing the editor waits for pending writes, and a failed or incomplete write keeps the editor open with a retry message. Creating a task still requires Save. While editing, the Extra tab shows a compact history with the task creation and latest modification timestamps. Shared-project assignee controls display member names instead of internal IDs, and a new task in an accepted shared project is assigned to its creator by default in both quick and full creation flows.

Task-card overflow actions are exclusive within a task list: opening the menu on another task closes the previously open menu.

A task card shows only the details the task has: the project button always, and description, tags, repetition, reminder and subtask icons when they are set. Shortcuts for adding a missing description, tags, repetition or subtasks appear while the card is hovered or holds keyboard focus; touch screens use the task editor. On narrow cards (phones) Edit and Pin move into the More actions menu, and a pinned task keeps its pin visible. Card dates, completion badges, reminder times and other app dates share one format: `17 Nov`, `17 Nov 2027` outside the current year, and `Tuesday 17 November` for a Weekdays day heading. In the task editor the description sits directly below the task name, separate from the date fields.

The task-card overflow menu also duplicates a task. The copy belongs to the user who made it and keeps the project, description, due date and time, repeat settings, priority, type, estimate, tags, assignees and subtasks, and its name gets a `[Duplicate]` prefix (added once, also when copying a copy). It starts open, active and unpinned, with all subtasks unchecked, no reminders and no recorded time.

### Status and completion

Tasks can be active or suspended. A suspension can remain in place until the user resumes the task or end automatically at a chosen date and time. The resume time defaults to `00:00` when the user selects a date without changing the time. Suspended tasks show a pause indicator instead of a completion checkbox, are excluded from active-work summaries, and are counted in the selected project's header across the currently included project hierarchy. The header count is a shortcut that selects the suspended-task filter. A database scheduler resumes due tasks every minute, while an in-app status clock updates an open view without requiring a reload. Every incomplete-task transition from suspended to active creates an in-app notification for the task owner and immediately queues an owner email through the transactional outbox; this covers both scheduled and manual resumes.

Open, suspended, completed, and all-task filters are available in project task lists. The open filter excludes suspended tasks. Completing a task records its completion timestamp; reopening it clears the timestamp. When completed tasks are visible, the card displays a `Completed 17 Nov` badge next to the task name (with the year outside the current one).

### Ordering and filtering

Project task lists support sorting by:

- priority, ascending or descending;
- due date, ascending or descending;
- creation date, ascending or descending;
- modification date, ascending or descending;
- name, A–Z or Z–A.

The chosen sort is remembered for each project in the browser (`tickist.project-sort.v1` in local storage), so it survives a reload; other devices and browsers keep their own choice, and a project without one uses priority, descending.

Tag views support tag selection, OR/AND matching, untagged-task mode, completed-task inclusion, search, pagination, and the same core ordering choices.

### Repeating tasks and reminders

Tasks support daily, workday, weekly, monthly, yearly, and custom recurrence (every N days, weeks, months or years). Completing a recurring task creates the next occurrence rather than leaving an ordinary completed item: the same task stays open with its next date. Each completed occurrence is recorded in `task_occurrence_completions` (migration 0042) with the person, the database time, the occurrence's due date and the time spent on it, so Done today, statistics and the email summaries count it as completed work. The web app and both MCP backends record it when they advance the task. The next date counts from the completion day, or, when the task repeats from its task date (Deadline or Scheduled day; stored as `from_repeating = 1`), keeps that cadence: it is the first occurrence after today counted from the task date, so a weekly task due 21 November and completed on 23 November moves to 28 November, and one overdue by several weeks skips the missed occurrences. The task editor reads and writes the task date as a day in the account timezone, the same midnight the recurrence stores; older dates saved as UTC midnight keep their calendar day. Workday repeats skip Saturday and Sunday; monthly and yearly repeats keep the calendar day and use the last day of a shorter month (31 January → 28 February, 29 February → 28 February). Migration 0041 stores the unit in `tasks.repeat_unit` (`day`, `workday`, `week`, `month`, `year`) beside `repeat_interval`, which stays a whole number of days (30 per month, 365 per year) for older clients; a trigger infers the unit for writers that send only days, and existing recurring tasks were backfilled the same way, so earlier monthly and yearly tasks now follow calendar months and years. Tasks saved as Daily (workweek) before the migration were indistinguishable from Daily and stay daily. Reminders store a date, time, timezone, and delivery state. A reminder is saved only once its date and time are in the future, so the task editor's automatic saving never stores the intermediate dates a date field passes through while the year is typed; migration 0039 enforces the same rule in the database with five minutes of clock tolerance.

MCP clients describe a repeat as `{ every, unit, from }`, where `unit` is `day`, `workday`, `week`, `month` or `year` and `from` is `completion_date` or `due_date`; the older `{ interval_days, from }` form still means plain days. Task results report `every`, `unit` and `interval_days`. Completing a recurring task through MCP advances the same task and resets its steps. MCP clients can also suspend incomplete tasks indefinitely or until a future timestamp and resume them explicitly.

MCP clients can read a time report for up to 367 days, grouped by root project, tag, or task, with the caller's minutes and everyone's minutes per group, task, and day in the account timezone. They can list, add, edit, and delete the caller's own time entries; entries follow the database rules (end after start, at most 24 hours, not in the future, no overlap). A task's spent minutes are a database-maintained total, so MCP `update_task` no longer changes them.

Each account stores an IANA timezone. New registrations capture the browser timezone, falling back to `Europe/Warsaw`; existing accounts are initialised to `Europe/Warsaw`. The web app and MCP both use this timezone, not the browser's, to determine the completion day and to set the next due date at local midnight.

## Focus views

- **Dashboard:** the sidebar Dashboard link shows the number of open tasks for today, pinned ones included (the same number as Weekdays → Today); today, overdue, next-action, and need-info work; pinned tasks are listed under Today (and not under Overdue) instead of in a group of their own; project next-action coverage. Each task group heading shows its total estimate and time spent next to the count when they are above zero. Today and Overdue compare calendar days in the account timezone, so a task due today is never overdue. A Done today group at the bottom lists tasks completed today and the occurrences of recurring tasks completed today (read-only rows with the next date and the time spent on that occurrence), and is hidden when there are none; completing a task records its completion time, and reopening clears it. When Today is empty and tasks are overdue, a button moves all overdue tasks to today in one step, keeping each task's time of day in the account timezone.
- **Task tree:** project-oriented task grouping for scanning a wider workspace.
- **Statistics:** completed work, late completions, overdue open work, and inactive projects over a time window. Completed occurrences of recurring tasks count as completions, and as late ones when completed after their due date.
- **Tags:** cross-project filtering and ordering.
- **Time:** a day report of time worked; see [Time tracking](#time-tracking).

## Time tracking

Work on a task is recorded as time entries, each with a start and an end. Durations are computed when read. A day is the calendar date of the entry's start in the account timezone, so the app, the daily email and MCP show the same numbers.

- **Focus mode:** the ▶ button on an incomplete task card starts a timer and opens `/app/focus/:taskId`, a screen outside the app shell that shows only the task, its project and the clock. The clock counts the focus session, which can span several time entries. Below the buttons it shows the time spent on the task today and in total; the total is the task's spent time (as on the task card, so the current occurrence of a recurring task) plus the running clock, followed by the estimate when one is set. Pause is a break: it closes the current entry, keeps the screen open and the clock at the paused time, and Resume starts a new entry while the clock continues from that time (a paused session survives a reload in the same browser tab, in `sessionStorage` key `tickist.focus-session.v1`). Stop and leave ends the session, running or paused, and returns to the previous view with the task still open, so the next start counts from zero; starting another task or completing the task also ends it; Complete task closes the entry, marks the task done (a recurring task is rescheduled) and returns to the previous view. Each button explains itself in a tooltip. "Back to tasks" returns to the previous view and leaves the timer running. Completing the timed task anywhere else in the app (for example its checkbox on the dashboard) also stops the timer. A person has at most one running timer; starting on another task stops the first, and a navbar chip links back to the running timer. Focus always belongs to a task.
- **Limit:** Settings → Focus sets an optional limit (default 50 minutes, 1–600) or none. The limit counts the whole focus session, including time before a pause. When a running timer reaches its limit, the browser plays a chime, shows a notification if permitted, and the clock changes colour; the timer keeps running until Stop. A timer left running is closed at 24 hours.
- **Manual entries:** the task editor lists the user's own entries and lets them add, edit or delete one with a date, a start and an end time in the account timezone. An end earlier than the start means work past midnight. Entries cannot overlap, be in the future, or last longer than 24 hours.
- **Spent time:** a task's spent time is the total of all people's closed entries plus undated legacy minutes from before time tracking. For a recurring task it counts only the current occurrence: entries started since its latest completed occurrence, which keeps the time of the occurrence it closed. The time report still lists every entry. It is maintained by the database and cannot be edited directly. Once time is recorded, the task card shows a `⏱ spent / estimate` chip (or only the spent time without an estimate) with a thin bar for the share of the estimate used; the chip turns orange past the estimate.
- **Time report:** `/app/time` shows a day, a Monday-to-Sunday week or a calendar month (previous/next move by the whole period, and the date picker selects the period containing that date) grouped by root project, with subproject tasks rolled up, or by tag. Each row shows "Me" and "Everyone"; when nobody else logged time that day, a single "Time" column replaces them. The week view adds a column per day with the user's own time and labels the last column "Total". Selecting a task opens its time entries below the row for adding, editing and deleting; new entries start on the shown day (or today within a week or month), and the report reloads after each change. A task with several tags is counted under each tag. The table can be copied into a spreadsheet.
- **Project summary:** the project header shows a `⏱ spent of estimated` chip with the totals of every task in the current project scope (subprojects included as selected, completed tasks included), with a thin bar for the share of the estimate used; it turns red past the estimate and is hidden when nothing is estimated or logged.
- **Sharing:** collaborators on a shared project log their own time on its tasks. Each person sees only their own entries; other people's work appears only in "Everyone" totals.

Entries are written through table policies or the `time_entry_start` / `time_entry_stop` RPCs, which use the database clock. `npm run time-tracking:test:db` applies every migration to a disposable Postgres container with stubbed `auth` and `storage` schemas and runs `tools/time-tracking/time-tracking.integration.sql` in a rolled-back transaction. It never uses an application database URL.

## Sign-in and account security

- The submit buttons of the sign-in, sign-up, password-reset and new-password forms stay enabled while the form is incomplete; submitting shows the errors of the missing fields. They are disabled only while a request runs, before the captcha is ready, or when the form cannot be used (no published legal documents, an expired reset link). The fields have visible labels and no sample-value placeholders.
- Sign-up, sign-in, password-reset requests, and the current-password check before a password change show a Cloudflare Turnstile check when the public `NG_APP_TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY` is configured (production sets it in `wrangler.toml`). The token is sent to Supabase Auth, which verifies it when captcha is enabled in the hosted project. Each token is used once and the check resets after every request. Without the key (local development and E2E) no check is shown. If the Turnstile script cannot load, the form explains this and offers a retry.
- Email links (sign-up confirmation, password recovery) carry a session that the application applies itself. When a different account is already signed in, `/auth/switch-account` asks whether to stay signed in or sign out and continue; a link never silently replaces the current account. Password recovery mode starts only after a recovery link was applied.
- Supabase Auth keeps the first password when an unconfirmed address signs up again. A repeat sign-up or resent confirmation more than two minutes after the first one clears that password, so the address owner confirms the account and sets a password with "Forgot password".

## Settings and portability

Settings cover profile data, avatar, password changes, email notification preferences, backup/restore, and API tokens. Export/import uses stable IDs and modification timestamps to make data transfer and duplicate handling reliable.

Version 2 backups include workspace definitions and project assignments. Version 1 backups remain importable and place imported projects in Private.

Interface icons (navigation, task-card actions and details, step and reminder controls, menus, sheet tabs, the theme switch and the header) are Lucide line icons; icon-only buttons have accessible names. The Simple view switch appears in the project header (stored on the project), in the day views and on the dashboard (each remembered in this browser under `tickist.task-view.v1`), and each Settings section has its own heading drawn in the current text colour instead of emoji; project icons come from the same set. Form placeholders and secondary text use one muted slate colour (`--tickist-muted-color`) that meets WCAG AA contrast on light and dark surfaces. Primary-coloured text uses `--tickist-primary-text`, a lighter indigo in the dark theme. The app has one accent colour, the theme's indigo, for primary buttons, the floating add button, selected options and tabs, and the active sidebar item; the sidebar marks the current page with `aria-current`.

## Installed app

Tickist can be installed as an app from supported browsers. The installed app opens the workspace at `/app`, or sign-in when no account is signed in, and its launcher offers New task and Time report shortcuts. Icons include a padded maskable variant so platform masks do not cut the logo, and the install prompt shows desktop and phone screenshots.

A service worker caches the application shell, so the installed app still opens without a connection. Task data is not stored offline: while the browser is offline, a notice says that changes will not be saved until the connection returns.

## Notifications

Users can receive daily or weekly email summaries according to timezone-aware preferences. The daily summary also shows the time worked that day, for the user and for everyone, grouped by root project; it is left out when no time was logged. Task reminders and notification digests use a database-backed email outbox and server-side workers; the browser does not send SES email directly.

## Account deletion

Settings links to `remove-account@tickist.com` to request permanent deletion. An operator verifies the request and uses the guarded deletion tool. Shared work requires review first; backups follow a separate retention process. This is not immediate self-service erasure. See [account deletion](account-deletion.md).

## History retention

Pending project invitations expire after 30 days; accepted memberships remain active. Notifications are retained for 180 days and MCP/activity history for 90 days, processed by daily cleanup after migration 0025. See [data retention](data-retention.md) for rollout and limits.

## Registration documents

Signup links to the exact published terms and privacy version, requires an unchecked terms acceptance checkbox, and records the version with a server timestamp. Documents are public and downloadable. With no final release, the new form disables signup; migration 0026 also rejects ordinary unconfirmed signup without a valid current acceptance. Migration 0027 publishes the Polish release 2026-09-30.1 from application-owned Markdown at deployment time. See [registration and legal documents](legal-registration.md) for publication and email-confirmation requirements.

A short banner asks about both optional analytics services at once, with equal Accept all and Reject all buttons and a privacy policy link; Customize keeps a separate switch for each service. Optional Cloudflare Web Analytics, including signed-in views, starts only after consent. Refusal preserves feature access. Privacy settings allow changing each choice; withdrawal reloads to stop measurement. The choice lasts 180 days and is checked when the application opens. Operational Worker logs remain separate.

Google Analytics 4 is separate and optional: its tag loads only after explicit Google consent and publication of privacy release 2026-09-30.2. It sends fixed route templates, sign_up for successful new registrations, and app_open once per page lifetime; no account, task or project IDs, content, query strings or fragments are included. Referrers retain only allowlisted search/social origins. Existing Cloudflare consent never enables Google. Advertising consent is denied and automatic enhanced measurement is disabled. Google consent and non-renewing analytics cookies expire after 180 days; withdrawal disables collection, deletes the host cookies and reloads. Google remains disabled in environments without NG_APP_GA4_MEASUREMENT_ID.
